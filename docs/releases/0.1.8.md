# Harbor 0.1.8

An update to Harbor 0.1.7. Harbor has a new tab, Ideate, for building small projects from an idea, and a new setup step that picks the extra tabs to suit you. Setup asks for fewer permissions and the rest are asked when a task first needs them. Brief is quicker to load and more accurate about what is still waiting on you, Harbor relaunches faster, sends are safer, and a round of security fixes closes more gaps found in testing. Everything in [0.1.7](0.1.7.md) still applies.

The first time 0.1.8 opens, it does a one-time fresh start of Harbor's own settings (see "A fresh start" below). Read that before you install.

## What's new in 0.1.8

### Ideate, a new tab

- **Describe a small project and Harbor builds it.** Ideate keeps each project in its own folder, shows what it is doing as it goes, and asks before anything risky. A command that reaches the network, or a change outside the project folder, always gets a card first, and no answer counts as no.
- **Five starting points:** General ideator, UI developer, Debugger, CS student, and Scripts & data. Each one changes how Ideate starts and how big its steps are. You can still ask for anything.
- **Ideate uses the built-in engine and one model, Inkling 2 Mini.** While a build runs, Harbor's other models are unloaded so it has room. Ask does not swap the model out from under a build: until it ends, Ask says Ideate is using the model and to ask again when it finishes or to stop the build. Inkling is let go a minute and a half after the build ends. If the Mac does not have enough free memory to load Inkling, Ideate says so in plain words and loads nothing.
- **If you already have Inkling 2 Mini from oMLX,** Ideate offers to use that copy without using extra disk space.
- **More GPU memory, when you want it.** If macOS's GPU memory limit is low, the empty Ideate tab shows "Ideate needs more memory for the GPU" with the one Terminal command that raises it. The command goes through the usual "Run in Terminal" card, so nothing runs until you press the button on the card. Terminal asks for your password, not Harbor, and the change goes away when the Mac restarts. Skip hides the row until Harbor restarts.
- **Long-running commands stop on their own.** Every command Ideate runs has a time limit, and its test loops have limits on time and number of tries, so one that never ends cannot hold up the build.
- **Ask can hand a big build to Ideate.** When Ideate is on, a request in Ask such as "build me an app that..." with a longer description opens Ideate with it. A short question about code stays in Ask. Type `/ideate` before an idea to always send it there, or `/ask` to always keep it in Ask.

### Extra tabs and the new "You" setup step

- **Ask, Brief and Day are always there. You can add up to two extra tabs** from Meet, Study and Ideate. A third is refused with "Remove one to add" the tab you pressed.
- **A new "You" step in setup asks who you are:** a student (high school, college, or grad school and research), someone who builds things (with five kinds), someone whose day is full of meetings, someone who manages a team, someone who writes or creates, home and life, or just the basics. Each answer suggests one or two tabs, and for people who build things it also sets what Ideate starts with. It is one choice kept on this Mac, and it sends nothing anywhere.
- **Settings has an "Extra tabs" row** that says which tabs are on ("Study and Meet are on", "None added yet") and how many of the two you have used. Ideate's starting point can be changed there too.
- **If you have never chosen,** Meet and Study stay on, as before.
- **Study says where its material is written.** Choosing Study in setup tells you its material is written by a cloud model with your own OpenRouter or Gemini key, kept in your Keychain and used for Study only.

### Onboarding

- **Seven steps:** Model, Permissions, Google, About you, You, How to use, and Get more. A step you finished shows a check, and you can jump between steps from the bar.
- **How to use** is a picture of the open notch with the tabs you picked.
- **Get more is the last page.** It offers Full Disk Access first, then Messages automation and Mail and Notes automation, all optional. Below them, "Try asking" shows up to four questions built from your own data once Harbor has some, or the general ones if not. Clicking one finishes setup and opens Ask with it typed in.
- **Day to calendar, asked once.** Right under Calendar in Permissions, Harbor asks whether the blocks it plans for you (study blocks and to-dos you kept) may be put on a calendar you pick. It is off until you say yes. Harbor writes only its own blocks, never changes an event of yours, and leaves alone events written by another Mac running Harbor.
- **"Let Harbor act for me" is asked at the moment it matters.** A short question appears on a card the first time you tap something Harbor could have done itself. "Not now" holds it for a week. Harbor never sends anything by itself.
- **Ask's starting questions come from your own data,** up to four, each only when what is behind it was read recently. Otherwise Ask shows its general ones.

### A fresh start

- **The first launch of 0.1.8 does a one-time fresh start of Harbor's own settings.** Setup and the "You" step begin again at step 1. Your "Act for me" choices, working and quiet hours, access mode, standing "always allow" choices and when Brief last went out are reset.
- **Nothing is deleted.** Those settings are moved into a dated folder under `~/.moe/backups`, and Harbor's preferences are saved there first. Your chats, memory, Google sign-ins, models and the Mail, Calendar and Messages caches stay.

### Permissions, asked when they are needed

- **Setup's Permissions step asks for three: Calendar, Reminders and Contacts** (Contacts so people show by name, not number). Each one is optional and nothing is asked until you click it.
- **Full Disk Access is on the last page,** with the two automation rows, all optional.
- **Harbor asks for the rest when a task first needs them.** Messages: the first time you approve a text, with the panel stepping aside while macOS asks and coming back. Notifications: the first time Harbor has an alert for you. The microphone: the first voice ask. Screen Recording and Accessibility: the first time you ask about your screen or read school pages in Study.
- **Settings > Privacy > Permissions... still lists them all,** so you can allow any of them ahead of time.
- **Before setup is finished,** Brief shows one row, "Finish setup", that opens the whole permission list. It does not name a source twice. After setup, Brief shows nothing about permissions, and Day shows one quiet line only when it cannot read Calendar or Reminders.
- **A grant is noticed without a restart.** Calendar and Reminders show Allowed as soon as you allow them, and Allow asks macOS directly. Start over clears a grant that was saved for an older copy of Harbor.

### Smoother notch and tab switching

- **Typing in Ask no longer redraws the whole panel,** so typing stays smooth with a long conversation open. The microphone's level meter does the same.
- **The panel takes the keyboard when you open it,** by a shortcut or a click, so you can type at once. Closing it hands the keyboard back.
- **Switching between Brief and Day shows today at once.** Harbor keeps today's last plan on this Mac, so opening Brief from another day in Day no longer shows "Loading your day...". The panel resizes once.
- **A burst of changes is one read,** not several running side by side.

### Faster relaunch

- **Quit & Reopen is quicker.** Harbor answers a quit from System Settings at once, so macOS no longer drops it and leaves the old copy running. The built-in engine and the browser connection stop side by side.
- **The notch is on screen sooner,** and Day shows the last plan from the previous launch at once, faded, until the fresh one is read. That plan is today's only, is kept in Harbor's own folder, and Start over removes it.
- **A reopen puts you back where you were.** If the Harbor window was up, it comes back, with Settings on the same pane.
- **Harbor's background service restarts itself** if it stops unexpectedly (after 1, 5, then 15 seconds), instead of staying down until you press Retry.

### Faster Day and Brief

- **Day and Brief load from what the service already holds,** and work that was repeated (the day's timeline was built three times) is done once.
- **Day does not claim an empty day too early.** While Reminders or Calendar are still coming in, Day speaks only for what it has read. It never says "nothing is due" or "nothing planned" before it knows.
- **Switching days in Day is instant.** Day keeps the last read of the days in its week row and shows a day you tap at once, then swaps in a fresh read only if that day changed.
- **Day's count of what is due stays steady.** It comes from today's last known plan, so reloading, switching tabs or looking at another day no longer drops it to zero for a moment.
- **After a long absence, Brief says it is still building.** After a night or a day away, Harbor waits up to three minutes for each source to come in (mail, texts, Calendar, Reminders, and your reply drafts). It shows "Brief is still building..." and lines such as "Calendar is still coming in", never "nothing needs you", and then swaps in the full Brief with a short fade. A source that has not reported anything in 90 seconds, or one that needs you to reconnect, is not waited for, and Brief names it instead. This happens once per absence, and a wake from sleep starts it again.

### Brief is more accurate

- **Things you handled yourself leave Brief.** A card, to-do or row about a conversation goes away once you answer it yourself, by a text in Messages or mail sent from Gmail, Mail or your phone. A to-do that stays is marked "you replied" with the day you answered, and nothing older than a week gets that label. A reminder or event card leaves once the same thing is in your own Reminders or Calendar.
- **Fuller reply drafts.** A draft is written from the newest message nearly whole, what was said before it (your own replies included), and whether the days it mentions look open on your calendar. Only busy times are read, never event titles. The longer material is used only when the model writing the draft runs on this Mac. Codes, keys, passwords and card or ID numbers are masked, and a message that still holds one is left out. A draft is never sent without you.
- **A reply card says what it answers.** Email and text reply cards show one muted line from the message they answer: its subject, then the line the reply is about, in quotes. Codes, addresses, links and numbers in it are masked, and a sensitive message shows only its subject or nothing at all.
- **Drafts never treat an old message's "today" as today.** A reply to a message from an earlier day is written knowing which day that message meant, and a draft that still says "today", "tonight" or "tomorrow" for it is written again or left for you. A reply card for a time that has already passed is not offered.
- **Brief waits for Ask.** Brief's model work (sorting, drafts, summaries, the first build) starts only while Ask is idle, so Ask answers faster. A call already running is never cancelled, and one that has waited a minute goes anyway so a long chat cannot starve Brief.
- **Plans in messages are read more carefully.** A day of the month ("on the 28th"), a half of the day ("6 tomorrow morning", "7 tonight") and a yes or a counter-offer in a reply are understood better, and a reading that is only a guess is shown as a guess.
- **Newsletters and lists no longer look like people.** A message addressed to a list, or a person's reply that happens to quote a newsletter, is told apart from bulk mail.
- **Different people under one subject stay separate.** Two people who wrote under "Question" are two conversations.
- **Open conversations come first.** When Brief has to cut a list, unanswered ones are kept before answered ones, then unread, then newest.
- **Old reminders are read in your time zone.** A reminder about an event that has already passed is judged by the day it is for you. Undated reminders about a week old get one "keep or delete?" question, and ones older than that are not used.
- **"Reading Mail..."** appears in Brief while Harbor reads a Mail it opened itself (see below).

### Send safety

- **A confirmed send stays sent after Stop.** If a text or an email reply went out and you press Stop, or the request stops before its answer is shown, Harbor records that it was sent, says so, and tells itself not to send it again.
- **"Might not have sent" is said plainly.** When a send was attempted and the turn stopped without a confirmed result, Harbor says its outcome is unknown and to check before asking for another send. A reply card in Brief that cannot confirm its send says "Harbor couldn't confirm the reply. Check Mail before trying again." (or Messages for a text), and the same send is not offered again.
- **A text Messages marks Not Delivered says "Not delivered."** Harbor does not say Sent for it and does not retry it.
- **Forward through Apple Mail works.** The approval card shows the email's subject and sender. Harbor checks the forward before it sends: the original is kept with any note above it, the recipients are exactly the ones you approved, and the sending address belongs to the email's account. It says "Forwarded" only after Mail confirms.

### Mail when Google is off

- **If no Google account is connected, or Google cannot be reached, Harbor still reads Mail.** If Mail is open and Automation is allowed, it reads it as before. Otherwise, with Full Disk Access, it reads Mail's own index.
- **Harbor can open Mail hidden to fetch new mail,** since Mail only fetches while it runs. It does this at most every 30 minutes (once an hour outside your working hours, weekends included), only when the cache is stale, never while the Mac is offline, and never within 15 minutes of you quitting Mail yourself. It needs Automation or Full Disk Access to read afterward and asks for nothing. It waits for Mail to finish fetching (up to five minutes), then reads, then quits only a Mail it opened, only if you never brought it forward.
- **A Mail account named "Google" is matched to its Gmail address** by the address macOS signed it in with.
- **Brief says "Reading Mail..."** while this happens, and never calls the inbox empty then.

### Cloud requests mask personal details

- **Before a request goes to a cloud model, Harbor swaps emails, phone numbers and card-like numbers for placeholders** such as [EMAIL_1], and does the same for your own name and your saved contacts' names. Replies, streamed text and tool arguments are swapped back on the Mac before Harbor shows or acts on them. The mapping lives only in memory, is never written to disk, and is never sent.
- **This reduces what leaves the Mac. It is not a guarantee.** It finds things by pattern, and it can miss some or mask more than it should.

### The built-in engine on oMLX 0.8.3 (preview)

- **The built-in engine follows the oMLX that is installed, now 0.8.3.** Harbor's memory-safety edits to the engine were re-checked against it and applied unchanged. This is a preview.
- **The engine's memory ceiling is 90% of the Mac's memory,** where it used to follow free memory and left big models little room to read a prompt. A ceiling you set yourself in oMLX's own settings is kept.
- **The built-in engine lists every model on the Mac,** including ones you already have in oMLX's usual folders, not only the ones Harbor downloaded. A model Harbor manages wins over one with the same name.

### Models settings

- **Two menus replace the old list: "General use" and "Coding".** General use covers chat, Brief, mail, planning and tool use. Coding covers writing and fixing code. Each menu changes its roles in one save, and a role tied to the cloud or to a connected app keeps that tie.
- **Qwen 3.5 9B is listed first in Coding** when it is on your Mac.
- **Research follows the Coding model** unless you gave it its own.

### Bundled skills

- **Four skills come with Harbor:** inbox clean-up (proposes archive groups for newsletters and old notifications, one group at a time, archived only after you say yes), meeting prep (a short brief for your next or a named meeting, read-only), week review (last week's events, mail sent and reminders still open, plus next week's load, read-only) and file tidy (plans how to sort a folder, Downloads by default, shows the moves and moves nothing until you confirm, and never deletes).
- **A skill that appears on its own is off.** A skill added outside Harbor loads switched off until you turn it on in Settings, and one that changes after you approved it is switched off again.
- **A skill's folder scope shows on its card,** and nothing it does can widen it.

### Ask ranks big results

- **When a tool's answer is too big, Ask keeps the rows your question is about,** not just the first ones. Mailbox reads no longer keep only the oldest mail. It drops what the model never uses first (long recipient lists, tracking parts of links, quoted reply chains), keeps every id a follow-up needs, and ends with one line saying what was left out, such as "412 more emails not shown".
- **Ranking happens on this Mac,** with no model call.

### Learn from my use

- **A private log of your requests on this Mac, on by default.** When the Mac's own engine answers in Ask, Harbor keeps the exchange (the messages as sent, the tools used and the reply) on this Mac, with credentials removed. Nothing is trained and nothing is sent. The log is capped at 200 MB and 90 days, and is left out of Time Machine.
- **Turn it off, or delete the history, in Settings** under "Learn from my use".
- **Writing and habits, off by default.** A separate switch in the Learned window, "Learn how I write", lets Harbor measure how you write from sent mail, messages, notes and reminder titles. It reads text in memory only and keeps counts and a short fixed list of greetings and sign-offs, never your words. It also shows when you send and finish things, and how you use Harbor (counts of tabs, tools and local versus cloud answers, never what you asked). Turning it off erases what it learned. It is used only for drafts written on this Mac, never for a cloud model.

### Google sign-in tokens in the Keychain

- **Google sign-in tokens are now kept in your login Keychain,** one item per account, instead of a private file. A token is moved only after it reads back from the Keychain exactly as written. If the Keychain is locked, denies access or does not answer, the token stays in the file as before and Harbor tries again on the next save or launch.
- **A sign-in saved by an earlier build is reused.** Harbor makes its own copy of it and never changes the original. A stale item is never used.
- **Disconnecting an account removes its item.**

### Security

These come from further independent testing of 0.1.7 and 0.1.8 builds. Details are left out on purpose.

- **Actions that could set up something to run later always ask first,** in every access mode and whatever you have allowed before. That covers login items, shell startup files, git settings, Harbor's own data folder and the installed app.
- **After Harbor has read text someone else wrote,** such as an email, archiving or marking mail, and adding reminders, ask first even in Full access.
- **Skills:** a skill added outside Harbor loads off, a changed skill is switched off again, a skill runs inside a sandbox with the folders its card names, and an oversized skill file is refused.
- **The phone gateway** uses its own narrow key, made when it starts and removed when it stops. It can reach only the routes the gateway uses, and an approval from a phone answers once and can never set a standing "always".
- **Credentials are redacted in more places,** and Harbor's activity record is kept to a size limit with several older copies, so a burst of actions cannot push out the record of an earlier one.
- **Google sign-in files are locked while they change,** so two copies of Harbor cannot overwrite each other.
- **Shell commands that name Harbor's local engine ask first,** with a card that says so.
- **Saved secrets are replaced safely,** so there is no moment where neither the old nor the new one is stored.

### Study and other fixes

- **Study has a Google account picker** for Drive when two or more connected accounts allowed Drive. Choosing Automatic goes back to the first one.
- **A teacher's outside link in Modules** is now offered with its own question and read only after you say yes, and a public caption or transcript file the same way.
- **Find allows material that is preparation for a midterm or final** by its title, while still refusing quizzes, tests and exams themselves.
- **Day's row of days starts on today,** with the next six days after it.
- **Smaller fixes:** research views stop reconnecting after about four minutes when a job is gone, an Ideate reply stays with the project that asked for it, an empty Memory list says what the filter means, and a delivery that does not go through puts the card back so you can answer again.

## For developers

- **The phone gateway works again.** It gets its own limited key each time it starts, so it no longer needs the app's key. Scripts that call the running app's service still need the app's key.
- **A Brief accuracy check is available.** It is read-only and prints counts only (no names, subjects, text or numbers). It compares what Brief shows against the Mail and Messages databases on the same Mac and reports false positives and an estimate of what Brief missed. It needs Full Disk Access, uses no network, and changes nothing.
- **Harbor measures its own launch and quit.** The unified log lists the milestones in milliseconds ("Harbor launch:" and "Harbor quit:").

## Requirements

- A Mac with Apple silicon (M1 or later). This download is for Apple silicon only.
- macOS 14 or newer. The built-in engine needs macOS 26.2 or newer; on macOS 14 to 26.1, Harbor needs another local engine running on the Mac (oMLX, Ollama, LM Studio or any OpenAI-compatible server).
- 16 GB of memory, and about 8 GB of free disk for the first model download.
- Ideate needs the built-in engine, the Inkling 2 Mini model (it is a large download) and about 18 GB of free memory when a build starts, so it is for Macs with more than 16 GB.
- For Study material: your own OpenRouter or Gemini API key. For slides read page by page: a Gemini API key.
- For Gmail, Calendar and Drive: a Google account on the app's test-user list.

## Before you install

- **Harbor is not notarized.** macOS blocks the first open; allow it in System Settings > Privacy & Security > Open Anyway.
- **Quit any other assistant build first.** Harbor keeps its data in `~/.moe` and its service on the local port 8765 (the built-in engine uses 8000). Another build using the same folder or port will conflict with it.
- **The first launch does a one-time fresh start of Harbor's settings.** Setup runs again, and a few settings are reset (see "A fresh start"). They are backed up, not deleted.
- **Google sign-in is in testing mode.** Only Google accounts on the test-user list can sign in, Google shows an "unverified app" warning, and sign-ins expire after 7 days.
- **Slides page reading needs Study's own connector** set to the Gemini API with a Study key. Harbor's other cloud settings do not turn it on.
- **Quit other big model apps before using Ideate.** Two engines holding large models on one Mac can run it out of memory.

## What was and was not tested

- **Automated checks:** the release build ran Harbor's full Simulation QA. All 755 checks passed: about 54,000 tests, none failed.
- **The Intel build** was made from the same changes. It has been checked only for x86_64 code; it has not been run on an Intel Mac.
- **Not tried by hand:** a full Ideate build from an idea to a finished project, the "You" step and the two-tab limit in the running app, Setup's three permission rows and the last page, the first approved text asking for Messages, the first alert asking for Notifications, Harbor opening Mail hidden when Google is off, forwarding an email through Apple Mail, moving an existing Google sign-in into the Keychain with macOS's own prompts, writing Day's blocks to a calendar, Stop after a real send, Quit & Reopen speed after granting Full Disk Access or Screen Recording, Brief's "still building" after a long absence, the Models menus, the bundled engine on oMLX 0.8.3 with real models, and the one-time fresh start on a Mac with an existing setup.

## Known issues

- **If the Keychain is locked or denies access,** a Google sign-in token stays in its private file until Harbor can save it there.
- **A text can fail after Messages first accepts it.** Harbor says "Not delivered" only once Messages marks it so; the red mark in the Messages app is the sign it did not go through.
- **A chat that was blocked by a failed text in an earlier build stays blocked.** Start a new chat.
- **Masking cloud requests is not a guarantee.** It works by pattern and can miss a detail or mask something harmless.
- **Ideate is for small builds.** It runs one build at a time, its prompts are kept short, and a long or many-step project can end with "ran out of memory room for this step". Ask a smaller step.
- **While Ideate is building, other models do not load.** Ask says Ideate is using the model; ask again when the build finishes, or stop it.
- **Forwarding with a note sends plain text** (the original's formatting is not kept), and an email with attachments cannot be forwarded with a note. Forwarding through Mail has not been tried by hand.
- **Learn from my use is on by default.** It keeps your requests on this Mac, with credentials removed, until you turn it off or delete the history.
- **Reduce Motion:** the four places where the panel could be briefly the wrong height should now cut straight to the final size. This has not been tried by hand.
- **A suggested block and a study block** can start at the same minute in Day.
- **If Calendar moves an event to a new time and re-issues it,** a study block can be booked twice.
- **A deck left reading** shows "Reading slides..." until Study is refreshed.
- **Lecture recordings are links only.** Harbor does not open or read them. A public caption or transcript file can be added after you say yes.
- **Find's never-open rule is still strict.** It refuses study material whose title contains words like "midterm" or "final", except a title that says it is preparation for one.
- **Find needs a course's Canvas address.** A course saved without one uses the Canvas site your other courses name, when they all name the same one. Otherwise open the course page and press "Read again" first.
- **Recent fixes to cloud-key handling** are covered by tests that stand in for the Keychain. They have not been tried by hand with every key and macOS prompt.
- **Some features are untried in the running app:** reading email and Messages attachments, meeting notes and writing-style drafts.
- **Study reads Canvas only.** Other school sites are not supported. Treat Study as untested against school sites and Drive files you have not tried yourself.
- **Reading the Mac's Mail and Messages** needs Full Disk Access in some cases.
- **Harbor does not update itself.**
- **The card for deleting a reminder is headed "changes your calendar".** The card below the heading names the reminder and its list correctly.
- **A schedule suggestion can appear at the top of Ask while an approval card is waiting** and push the approval card below the visible part of the chat. Scroll down to find it.
